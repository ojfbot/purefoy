Skyfall – MGM

 

SKYFALL – PRETITLE SEQs & MI6 SEQs
Looking at Lighting

 

BRONSON’S HOTEL ROOM

The opening scene of ‘Skyfall’, in which Bond is too late to save the life of agent Bronson, established the look of our main character and was essential to shoot before our second unit began work in Turkey on the action sequence that was to follow. The hotel room set was built on stage at Pinewood Studios well in advance of our shooting schedule, as the scene provided weather cover for the initial part of our shoot. This also allowed time for Sam to rehearse the scene with Daniel and for me time to light it. It wasn’t a difficult set to light, but I wanted to be sure that the set dressing would allow me to contrast a hard sunlight effect with a dark interior. The size of the windows and the treatment on them was crucial, hence the wooden shutters that allowed for a direct light source without revealing the exterior. Initially we considered using a backing outside the window, but I suggested the entire exterior be ‘blown out’ and only revealed once Bond exits the building into our Istanbul location. The direct ‘sunlight’ is from an Arri T12 and it is positioned above a white reflector that is ‘blown out’ using a series of open face 2K blondes. I used a T12 as it is a very sharp light even when quite close to a subject, as it was in this case. There was no light used inside the set, just some smoke haze.

The approach of Bond

 

Bronson’s hotel room was designed around some specific choices for the action as well as some preconceived shots, such as that opening the film in which Bond appears as a soft focus image instead of the more familiar and iconic sharp silhouette.

CU of Bond

 

The shots that follow were decided on during a blocking rehearsal with Daniel. Key was the ‘blend’ between the set and our location in Turkey. We knew we wanted to follow Bond in one shot; along a passageway, down some stairs and into a crowded street, so the shot where we left the set and the shot in which we picked up the action in Istanbul were important in making this transition work.

Bond & the dead agent

 

Silhouette of Bond in hotel room

 

The scene breaks here from stage to location.

Pan from Interior to Exterior

 

Bond exits into Istanbul

 

Sam and I had storyboarded the entire pre-credit sequence of ‘Skyfall’ and from those storyboards we had made a pre-vis sequence that incorporated both locations we had scouted as well as camera angles and lens choices. I had never been involved in such detailed pre-vis work on any other production before, but it was essential, given the complexity of the scene and the fact that much of the stunt work would be shot by a second unit. Most of this second unit work had to be done with a double not only for safety reasons but also because the stunt team needed time to work out the intricacies of the action. Besides, there were few occasions when Daniel was not working with first unit. First unit had always been scheduled to follow on from 2nd unit and to fill out the sequence in Istanbul with specific angles on Daniel but, given that the stunts and effects involved had now been so well worked out, we shot many more angles than we had originally intended.

The beach bar

 

MI6 HEADQUARTERS

 

Looking out from MI6 Headquarters

 

This was a set and a Translight backing was made from a photograph that was taken looking across the river from a window in the real MI6 building. The photograph was taken on a grey day as we wanted the look of London to contrast with the bright and colorful world that Bond was in. I used an overhead pattern of Spacelites to create a soft top light through the set’s two large skylights but also to give some feeling of natural daylight on the outside of the window frames and exterior patio. The side windows were frosted and, to light them, I used a second layer of diffusion with Maxis beyond. This double diffusion blew out the windows themselves but only allowed minimal soft light onto the set, the Spacelites lighting through the skylight being the major source. The Translight was illuminated using Image 80s but above this we placed a row of 10K Fresnel lamps to push light through the main set window and to back light the onset rain effect. I used the multiple direct lamps to create a soft source because that felt in keeping with the grey day whilst it also controlled any spill that might pollute the Translight.

MI6 Lighting Plan

 

MI6 – Lighting Section

 

MI6 Wide

 

MI6 Night

 

For the night shot, the Translight was still lit from behind using the Image 80s but with a reduced intensity and with additional blue gel. From the rear and from the front, I used small Fresnel lamps to highlight certain areas with a warm or a cool light using a combination of CTO, 013 straw or blue gel. All the lamps were spotted in to light specific windows or wash a building from the back of the Translite or, from the front, as if warmer streetlights were pooling in the areas being lit. Otherwise, the shot is lit by the desk lamp alone, which was carefully chosen by the set dresser, Anna Pinnock.

 

WHITEHALL – MALLORY’S OFFICE

Mallory’s Office

 

Mallory’s office was a stage set and, rather than using a backing beyond the windows, we added shears to give some texture to the window in this profile shot. The scene primarily played out in angles that were parallel to the window, so I wanted to use a soft side light that utilized the entire window area. Besides that, it seemed that this profile angle worked better without detail behind the characters, which would have only interfered with the clean lines of their profiles. Exterior to the window and about 5’ beyond it is a large light grid cloth whilst another 12’ beyond that is a row of 10K Fresnel lamps. There was a second layer of diffusion on the window glass that helped spread light more evenly across the room but also enhanced the ‘fall off’ of that light across the room.

 

M16 BUNKER

 

Our Production designer, Dennis Gassner, created the MI6 bunker complex using a combination of locations and sets. The largest set was built on the 007 stage at Pinewood and this included a passageway into the main underground space. The tunnel into which Bond is driven and the unassuming entrance door that leads into the MI6 bunker was a location in South London as were a number of individual sets in which Bond is seen getting back into shape.

Car driving to MI6

 

This ‘tunnel’ was actually quite short so we used multiple passes and multiple camera angles to create the sensation that the SUV is travelling a far longer distance. The florescent fixtures we used here are the same as appear in the main bunker set that follows.

The passageway to MI6 bunker

 

The entrance into the MI6 bunker is through a meandering passageway that was built on set to accommodate the written dialogue. We lit this section with warm bulkhead lamps, as if these were left over from the Victorian era of underground London that we see far more of later in the story. These bulkhead style lights were fitted with 100watt tungsten bulbs that were dimmed down to make them even warmer in comparison with the cool fluorescent lights.

Reveal of MI6 Bunker

 

For the bunker, John Higgins, my gaffer, suggested we use a narrower T8 (1” diameter) tube rather than the traditional T12, as these would look a little more ‘high tech’ while still creating a soft source. Because of the large number of tubes we would need, well into triple figures by the time we had finished, we chose a cheaper ‘off the shelf’ brand for all of the location and stage work that were combined to form the MI6 bunker complex. Having tested multiple brands we settled on a 4,000K tube that gave us the cooler look but also had a minimal green tint. The green was not a problem as I did not intend to combine the florescent lighting with more than the odd practical source, such as those that light the entrance tunnel to the main overlook before the first introduction of the bunker. During prep, we also tested the dimming of these tubes so we could have greater control over the lighting of our closer shots. Often a light will dramatically change in color temperature when dimmed but luckily the units we chose would vary little when dimmed to their maximum, which was around 50%. Most fluorescent bulbs will flicker when dimmed so it is important to test what limits there are before the pressures of production might lead to an unseen problem.

The design of the lighting had to fit with Dennis’ overall concept for the set, but it also had to light the scenes that were taking place within it. To fulfill both requirements Dennis and I settled on a combination of straight rows of fixtures within the larger open spaces and square shaped fixtures for smaller spaces, such as the interview room.

Interview Room

 

Interview from behind the glass

 

The interview room overhead fixture was a simple square of double tube units that creates a soft overhead source, similar to a ring light. For this fixture the Art Department built aluminum coves for each tube, to ‘focus’ the light down, rather than sideways to the wall or up where it would bounce off the ceiling and soften the contrast of the shot. This was crucial on a set that was deliberately white.

Bunker at night

Each double pair of tubes in a row were dimmable so that it was possible to have fixtures on a high output at the back of a shot whilst dimming them down progressively towards the camera. In this image the foreground overheads are off completely and a practical lamp, that holds a matching bulb, is the sole light source.

Shooting range

 

The gym

 

M’s office within the MI6 bunker was built above the main vaulted space so it had a lower ceiling but was large enough that we could forgo the covers on the tubes and, by allowing the light to spill out to the sided and above the fixture, create a softer look from the one central fixture.

M’s Office

 

Bond in M’s Office

 

M’s Office/Bunker

 

There are two square fixtures above M’s desk, one a 6’ double tube unit and another a 4’ unit. Each fixture tubes could be dimmed individually so that the larger square could be set at a lower level that the central 4’ square.

Q’s Office

 

Q’s office was lit using the similar double tube fixtures and were recycled from the main set. The only additional lighting I used were similar fixtures mounted on stands that I might use for a close shot of an actor. Usually these were then dimmed down so as not to compete with the main overhead rig.

 

THE BARRACKS

 

The Barracks

 

We shot the scene with M standing over the coffins at the Naval College in Greenwich. At the same time we shot an exterior funeral scene in the same location, but this does not appear in the final cut.

The Barracks lighting plan

 

Using 18K HMI lamps I bounced light from a series of 20’ x 12’ Ultrabounce reflectors. I used a bounce source here for a soft light effect and, because the windows faced south, I needed to control any sunlight. It was also a simpler rig to bounce light for a soft effect rather than use direct sources, which would have needed scaffolding to reach an equivalent angle of light. The opposite windows were diffused but there was no light behind them as I wanted the simple one-sided soft source.
